# 18 September 2026 — The sheep harness and the collie references come out

The hosted-rc experiment (sheep's collie) was shut down, and running an
agent off the laptop is expected back in another form. So that the next
form is not read through the old one's names, the sheep harness and every
mention of the collie were removed from the code, the tests and the agent
guide, rather than left dormant.

**What went.** `packages/rc/src/sheep.ts` and `packages/cli/src/sheep.ts`,
with their six test files and the fake `sheep` binary. `--harness sheep` is
no longer a harness `isocan harness` finds or `isocan rc add` names. From
the room module: `RoomDeps.endSession` and `whereOf`, `RoomAdapter.place`,
`where` and `bornPass`, and the two extra arguments to
`RoomRows.setSessionId` — each existed for a session that outlives the
process, and the sheep was the only one. From the rc row: `sheep` and
`cellPass`. From the CLI: the cell's badge in `isocan badges`, the cell's
problem line in `isocan who`, the `cell` fence state, the sheep half of
both `remove` verbs, and the station host as a bench row's `runsAt`.
`isocan pass --agent` stays, and its printed hint no longer names
`collie new --pass`.

**What stayed, on purpose.** Everything the experiment left that is
generic: `DaemonRoutes` and the address helpers exported from `isocan/rc`,
`POST /api/rc/release`, the home-asked liveness of #306, and
`GET /api/projects/:id/passes/:passId`. That last route now has no caller
in the CLI; it is a deployed wire route and a minter's read of its own
pass, so it was left rather than removed in the same change.

**What was not touched.** The changelog, the closed `sheep-harness`
project and the research notes are records and read as they did; the four
that claimed something stands (`sheep-harness`, *Sheep as standing agents*,
*A sheep on two machines*, *The bearer is mostly built*) are now
`superseded` in their front matter, which is what the roadmap reads. The
design-partner baseline keeps `sheep.ts.txt`: it is a hashed snapshot of
source at a pinned revision, and the eval refuses a baseline whose hashes
moved. The farm theme's sheep cursor (#195) and the *sheepdog* mention-gate
design are different things and stay.

**A row that already says sheep.** An `rc-agents.json` row with
`"harness": "sheep"` is now an unknown harness: a summons for it fails with
the ordinary *no ACP adapter for harness "sheep"* line. `isocan rc add
<name> --harness <other>` re-enrols it; the row's old `sheep` and
`cellPass` fields are ignored. Nothing ends the sheep or the cell's badge
that such a row left behind — `sheep` and `isocan badges --kill` do that by
hand.

## The first minute is designed (#332)

An agent in a small hosted sandbox spends most of a person's minute on
isocan itself: 35 s installing 227 packages, 2 to 4 s starting the CLI for
every command, about 50k tokens of guide. A laptop hides all of it, so it
was measured rather than argued. `isocan --version` loads 456 modules; 297
are our own `.ts` files, 4.7 MB, transpiled or cache-checked by tsx on every
command, and all of `@isocan/server` and every module's guide load whatever
the command is. An esbuild bundle of the same CLI, built as an experiment and
thrown away, starts in 0.17 s against 0.29 s warm and 0.38 s cold. The
`release` branch is about 40 MB, of which `docs/` is 14 MB that no install
runs.

[`docs/projects/first-minute/`](../projects/first-minute/) is the project:
three journeys with a number each, the measurements and the changes (a
bundled release CLI, an install that resolves nothing, lazy loading, a guide
in tiers, a summons that carries its thread, an identity for an agent that
holds no secret), and a seven-phase walk. Nothing is built. Three decisions
are left open for Dimitri: a thin agent-only artifact, how the guide is cut,
and the shape of the no-secret identity.

## The room tells a host who a turn is for, and lets it hold one (#333)

Two small additions to `isocan/rc`, the first things #333 asks for that
wait on no decision. #333 itself narrowed the same day, in its comments: a
hosted place for agents is not a new party at the desk. A space at the host
is a machine of the person's that somebody else runs, joined by a pass they
mint, as a laptop is.

**`RoomTurn` carries `canvasId`, `agent` and `owner`.** The laptop reads
those from `cwd`, the environment and `isocan whoami`. A host that serves
many people has none of the three, and files its records and counts its
allowance by them. Additive; the laptop's adapter ignores them.

**`RoomHold`.** Anything a host threw from `adapterFor` or `open` was a
failed turn: the thread read "couldn't answer … `isocan rc`'s log has the
detail", and the room tried again every minute. For a spent allowance that
is the wrong sentence, about a log the person cannot read, repeated until
the reset. A host now throws `new RoomHold(line, retryAfter)`: the room says
`line` in the thread in the system voice, once, puts up no face, keeps the
summons pending and asks again at `retryAfter`, as it already does for the
guard's hourly ceiling. A held turn still counts as one of the hour's turns,
as a failed one does.

**Read, not changed: what ending a space's badge ends.** `killAndSweep`
expels every badge the dead badge passed onto a canvas, so ending a space
from the identity menu ends her sandbox badge there too
(`sweep.test.ts`, "ends the holder and sweeps every room"). With one
exception that is by design for machines and open for agents: where a link
grant is live on the canvas the sweep re-roots her badge onto the link
("does not un-invite"), and she stays admitted under her own name, at the
link's rung. Her badge claims only her, so it is not one of the person's
surfaces and they cannot end it themselves. Reported on #333; no fix chosen.
